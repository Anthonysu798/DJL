# Writing emails people answer

A good email is easy to read in thirty seconds and makes the next step obvious. Write for the reader, not for the sender.

## Before writing

Work out, from what the user gave you:

- **Who reads it** and how they relate to the sender (manager, customer, teammate, stranger).
- **The one thing it must achieve**: a reply, a decision, a yes, an apology accepted, awareness of a change.
- **The tone**: match the user's own message if they pasted one; otherwise friendly and professional.

If the purpose or reader is genuinely unclear, ask one short question. For small gaps (a name, a date) leave a clear blank like `[Name]` or `[date]` instead of guessing.

## Shape

1. **Subject line**: specific and short, saying what it is about and, if needed, what you need ("Budget sign-off needed by Friday"). Skip it for chat messages.
2. **First line**: the point or the request. No warm-up paragraph.
3. **Context**: only what the reader needs to act, in two to four short sentences or a few bullets.
4. **The ask or next step**: one clear request with a date if timing matters. Make it easy to answer ("A quick yes or no is fine").
5. **Close**: a short, warm sign-off.

Aim for under 150 words for most emails. Use bullets for lists of three or more items.

## By situation

- **Follow-up with no reply**: assume good intent, restate the ask in one line, offer an easy way out ("If this is no longer a priority, just let me know").
- **Tricky or upset message**: acknowledge their point in their own terms first, avoid blame and defensive words, state facts calmly, offer a concrete next step. Never match an angry tone.
- **Asking for something**: say exactly what, by when, and why it matters to them if it does.
- **Announcing a change**: what is changing, when, why it helps, what (if anything) people must do, and who to ask.
- **Saying no or giving bad news**: say it early and clearly, give the reason briefly, offer an alternative if there is one.

## Finish

- Read it as the recipient: is the ask obvious? Is anything that could sound cold, passive-aggressive or vague?
- Remove filler ("I just wanted to reach out", "per my last email") and stacked apologies.
- If the user might want options, offer one alternative version (for example a shorter or a warmer one) after the main draft.

Never send anything yourself. Give the user the text to copy, and say which blanks they still need to fill.
