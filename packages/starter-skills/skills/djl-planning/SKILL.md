# Planning that survives real life

A useful plan tells the person what to do next, in what order, and how they will know it is done. It is honest about time.

## 1. Understand the goal

Find out, from what the user said:

- **The outcome** in one sentence, and what "done" looks like.
- **The deadline** and any fixed dates.
- **Resources**: who is involved, budget, how many hours a week they really have.
- **Constraints**: things that must or must not happen.

Ask at most two short questions if the deadline or available time is missing, because they change everything. Otherwise state your assumptions at the top.

## 2. Break it down

- Split the outcome into three to six **phases** (for example Prepare, Do, Check, Finish).
- Under each phase list **tasks** a person can finish in one sitting, roughly 30 minutes to half a day. Split anything bigger.
- Start every task with a verb and make it concrete: "Book the venue for 40 people", not "Venue".
- Mark **dependencies**: which tasks need another to finish first. Put them in that order.
- Add a **checkpoint** at the end of each phase: a quick way to see it is really done.

## 3. Put it on a timeline

- Work backwards from the deadline. Put the riskiest and longest-lead items (bookings, approvals, deliveries, anything waiting on other people) first.
- Add a buffer of about 20% of the total time. Plans slip; say so plainly.
- Never schedule more hours than the person said they have. If it does not fit, say what to cut, delay or hand off, and let them choose.

## 4. Present it

Use this shape unless they asked for something else:

1. **Goal and deadline** in one line, plus your assumptions.
2. **This week**: the first three to five tasks, so they can start today.
3. **Full plan**: a table with Task, Owner, Start or due date, and Depends on, grouped by phase.
4. **Risks**: the two or three things most likely to go wrong and what to do if they do.
5. **Checkpoints**: the dates to stop and check progress.

Keep it readable: short lines, no jargon. If they want a file, save it as a spreadsheet (one row per task) or a Markdown checklist in the folder they choose, and tell them where it is.
